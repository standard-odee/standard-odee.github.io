Score: 4/5

I had this anime on repeat as background noise for months, I was a bit obsessed, I felt it was genius. I would hang on to its every word. Now that my passion has waned, I hold almost no feelings for it, other than a personal debt I owe it for keeping me company for a time. Looking back, this production could just be the result of an author's calculated writing which I incidentally fell in love with, but I feel some parts are more empathetic than calculated which makes TEIS (The Eminence in Shadow) such a fun isekai adventure.

A year or two before the anime debut, I had run into the fan translated LN online. The premise was intriguing, but I could not get past the first chapter, likely due to a poor translation. However, the first episode of the anime served as a better opener. Isekai stories usually jump straight into the main fare, a visit by Truck-kun and a quick flashback to establish their motivation is usually enough to get started. However, while Cid Kagenou's character is easy to understand, it's unconventional enough that it needs a full episode setup, or else risk leaving the viewers disoriented. It's the story of a man who is obsessed with becoming a mastermind behind the scenes. He just finds this archetype cool, becoming The Eminence in Shadow is the end goal in itself. His reincarnation into an isekai (lit: new world) gives him the chance to realize his dream in proper.

The narrative relies on the suspension of disbelief, and irony, such as Cid remaining unaware of being the leader of the powerful shadow organization he accidently created. Cid is in fact seldom aware of the full context in which he acts or the consequences of his actions, a convenient plot device. As Cid only acts in pursuit of his aesthetic ideal, his actions are meaningless, making for the perfect MacGuffin. Cid's aloofness is also played for comedy. In fact TEIS is funny enough to pass as a comedy on its own. The writer seemingly never gives up an opportunity to pen in a joke, keeping the downtime between acts entertaining and adding fun to the action. A minor gripe: the delivery is deadpan and some jokes are so subtle you'll miss them, but at times they're obnoxious and immersion breaking .

Sickness is a recurring theme in The Eminence in Shadow. The members of Shadow Garden were all former sufferers of an incurable disease that rotted their body. A man turns into a villain due to his daughter's sickness. Another is afflicted by malady just as he reaches the pinnacle of the martial world. Even the "beads of diablos" pills used by the Cult of Diablos to gain borrowed power are reminiscent of medication. Sickness is a common anime trope used primarily to garner sympathy. TEIS portrayal extends beyond that. First it doesn't view sickness through the eyes of the main character, "the sick girl confined to bed" isn't just there to motivate the protagonist. Sickness doesn't put a pause to people's lives, rather they must continue to live through it. And so TEIS doesn't linger on the dramatic elements of sickness, it doesn't sympathize but rather gives due respect to the character. Nor does it reduce sickness to a blameless affliction, but shows it to be a monster that will overwhelm a character and their motivations. This empathetic approach to sickness and its recurrence as a plot point makes me suspect the author draws from personal experience but I can't verify it through interviews so it remains conjecture.

As Cid is a self professed MOB character when he's not acting as the Eminence in Shadow, it is left to the secondary characters to play the lead. The result is an ensemble cast where the main perspective is the heroine's. If you've noted that I omitted hero, it's due to the lead roles being limited to women. In fact all the "good guys" with the exclusion of Cid are women, and all the antagonists are men. In typical anime fashion, all women fall for Cid despite him showing absolute zero interest in others. That said, contrary to convention, these heroines do not play the secondary lead to the average male LN protagonist. Upholding the embodiment of a strong independent heroine only until a convenient pretext arises for the hero to swoop in, save the day, and earn her unreserved affection. Rather, TEIS develops and advances the plot through it's heroine's independent perspective, we empathize and cheer for them; personally, I rather admire the tenacity and strength of character they exhibit.

TEIS is a funny enjoyable watch. The villains are cartoonish, but viewers will find their real life basis convincing. The heroines are heroic but have their own flaws and struggles we can empathize with. TES is not too serious that it stops being fun, but serious enough to be meaningful.

P.S. The English voice acting is fantastic, the humor depends on the delivery and timing which the voice actors do a fantastic job of.
