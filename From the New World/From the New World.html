Score: 4/5

Forward: It's been maybe 4 years since I watched this anime. I'm writing this review based on this memory, so I do not expect to do it justice. But I will write.

From the New Wolrd carefully layes out its world in plain site, with everything the viewer needs to finish writing the story on their own. In spit of which I was still caught by surprise. Akin to the metaphor of frogs slowly boiling in a pot, I didn't notice as the plot built up, only to find myself exhausted at the end of the multi episode climax. I attribute this to it's source medium, the novel, as it was reminicent of getting hooked by a novel for hours, unable to put it down.

The story structure and pacing is rare in tv anime. As the medium, along with its usual source material: manga and light/web novels, are periodic publications. This instills a requirement for each publication (chapter, episode, volume) to stand on its own, providing some sort of satisfaction or climax. Hence the monster of the week formula. Not necessarily a bad thing, but often this means adding extreous elements and structuring the story around the release schedule. The novel, in contrast, benefits from every detail set in relation to the whole.

From the New Wolrd is a coming of age story told from Saki Watanabe's perspective, set in a post apocalyptic world brought on by humans developping psychic powers. Following the precipitious end to her childhood, the events unfolding and the society she inhabits outpace her understanding. In her journy culminating in adhulthood, we see a mirror of our own experience. But beyond the narrow perspective of a child growing up, the anime refrains from passing judgment or taking an emotional stance. We are thus invited as viewers to join Saki in deciphering the world and reconicling society's dichotomies.
