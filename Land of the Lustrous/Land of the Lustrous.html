Score: 4/5

At the time, Land of the Lustrous was one of the only anime licensed by Amazon Prime, so I gave it a shot. Now, that I've rewatched it a few times and finished reading the manga (volume 11 really is ironically the better ending point); it's with embarrassment that I recall overlooking it due to its all 3dcgi production. Fortunately, a random clip showing off the animation convinced me otherwise.

Houseki no Kuni gets it's name from the anthropomorphized Gems, their entire world consists of the island they inhabit, the seas that surround it, and their beloved Sensei. The premise is succinct and the plot straightforward. As are the motivations and traits of the characters, in the relatively small population, we find a society that covers all the classic archetypes and contemporary eccentricities. In this minimalistic world, the author is able to precisely develop the story of Phos, a classic tragic hero.

Our protagonist Phos, the youngest and weakest of the gems, is imbued with an innocent curiosity and desire to understand her place in the world. It's difficult to imagine that, from the first episode, Phosphophyllite walks the path of the tragic hero. Principally it's a cathartic experience, but not a melancholy parade. The show keeps an airy atmosphere, at times almost detached from the tragedy unfolding. As we viewers, hope that Phos' suffering is not undue, and happiness will find her.
