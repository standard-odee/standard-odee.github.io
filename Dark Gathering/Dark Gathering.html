Score: 4/5

My first impression was one of discomfort. The exaggerated character design of Yayoi , both in clothes and features, had me expect a shonen story, where the main characters are never really in danger. This preconception clashed so hard in the first episode that I simply dropped it. I must say, I also gave up Berserk after reading the first chapter.

When I came back to it with revised expectations, I expected the discomfort, and I enjoyed it. It's a horror anime after all. I am not a fan of horror, as a kid I could not stand horror, my fight or flight instincts were so wound up I could not enjoy it. My mind was so affected even in my teens it was convinced zombies were hiding in the field at night, though the rationale side of me knew that was a ridiculous notion. It was bad enough, I completed some amateur exposure therapy by watching The Grudge on mute until I was comfortable with it. That experiment killed horror for me, when the visuals, and sounds no longer could create a visceral reaction in my primal brain, it simply wasn't scary anymore.

Dark Gathering is scary in a primal sense. Itself an incredible accomplishment for an anime, which by nature of the medium depends heavily on suspension of disbelief. We can go on about its technical success in length, but to what horror does it amount to? A typical work of horror is structured in two parts: mounting dread causing a flight response, followed by a climax where the fight is taken to the horror. Usually the first half is where the fear is concentrated, the slow mounting dread turning to horror. By the second half the dread has been realized, and the viewer acclimatized so it's harder to maintain the horror, which is why many works prefer a gun blazing triumphant cathartic climax rather than trying to keep their viewers scared. This isn't a wrong approach, that catharsis is the point of horror after all.

Dark Gathering is no different, it follows the paradigm described above, with a clever meta design. While the climax remains the triumphant victory over a spirit, taking the fight to the spirit in the first place is the source of horror. Eiko and Keitaro don't have to hunt the spirits, they could run away, they've already learned to live with their curses. But in making the choice to fight, by staying true to their convictions, they invite untold horrors. Usually, the decision to fight comes after we understand what we're dealing with, and have a path to victory, and perhaps most crucially, when we're cornered. Dark Gathering's terror lies in fighting something unexplained, with no certainty of victory, and for no compelling reason other than choosing to stay true to one's self.
